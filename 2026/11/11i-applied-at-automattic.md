## What’s motivating you to explore an engineering role at Automattic and why now?*

When I was a boy, we didn't have Wordpress. We didn't even have PHP.  We wrote all of our HTML by *hand*, and we liked it that way!

This is my ad from around 1997: https://b.robnugen.com/mrlabs/mr-labs-big.jpg

I've been a developer for most of my professional life. I've been working mostly in PHP for the past 15 years. I've got personal and client websites written in PHP, nurturing some of them from PHP 5.3 to now PHP 8+ for all of them.

As you may have noticed (ha!), AI tools are poised to transform the world. I want to stay connected with humans and learn more best practices even as I have heaps of agent harnesses working with and for me. In mid-2027, after 23 years in Japan, I will be moving from Tokyo Japan to Adelaide Australia, so I have begun an AI-powered job search process... within which Automattic showed up very high on the list of great matches for my work and style.

Until my AI-powered search, I had *NO* idea that Automattic tries carefully to keep humans first. I'm happy to hear this because I've been involved with heart-centered communication with MKP (ManKind Project) since 1997; I established a men's group in Tokyo [1] because I have a need for heart-to-heart communication with others!

[1] https://mkpjapan.org/

## Tell us about a project or piece of work you’re especially proud of 

TL;DR: AI and I are building a bilingual client website while emailing the client in Japanese language (and I don't speak biz level Japanese!).

A few years ago (pre-AI-era), I created a template[2] and process[3] for launching websites on Dreamhost Shared Hosting. I could spin up a new user  and database on DH with a new domain in about an hour, and then begin manually working on the project in VSCode (typing with my fingers like a pre-ai-caveman). 

Integrating this template and process with AI-agents (Claude Code), I have been able to work with a Japanese client: a cronjob runs every five minutes on my local machine, pings me on Telegram if the client wrote to me. Then I can sit at my terminal, and monitor Claude in translating the email and doing Test Driven Dev to make the changes to the site. Because of the magic of AI, I've been able to produce up-to-date documentation for the site in multiple languages and formats. This was nigh impossible just a couple years ago for me as a solo dev, especially while not able to speak Japanese at business level!

[2] https://github.com/thunderrabbit/new-DH-user-site
[3] https://github.com/thunderrabbit/new-DH-user-account

## How has AI changed your work — if at all?*

In addition to the story above, I made a game of Dots and Boxes in 1990 in *Pascal*[4] for IBM PC. Last month, Anthropic was like "hey we'll give you $250 credit if we can connect with your Github account," I was like *hmmm*! and found the old game and asked Claude to translate it to run online. I was so happy to see my old game again that I created a whole games website[5] including DOTS and some other games now playable over internet.

On my local laptop, I've got N+1 tabs in my terminal with different agent harnesses geared to the project I work on in that terminal. I say N+1 because at any given moment another idea will come up that I can quickly explore. I see lots of new ideas and possibilities coming available with AI; if we can guide them and guide ourselves to learn to work with AI in a good way, I think the world will be better able to make the transition to the post-AI era.

[4] https://github.com/thunderrabbit/dots-in-pascal
[5] https://games.chatforest.com/dots/

## What’s an idea, book, blog post, or talk that recently changed how you think about your craft?*

I've been listening to Peter Diamandis podcast [6] regularly speaking about all the possibilities and reasons to be optimistic; things are changing faster and faster. We as humans will have more options to re-invent ourselves multiple times per lifetime, per year, per day??  Where does it end? We humans still have to breathe and our hearts still beat on the order of seconds but the tech will keep diving into the domain of femtoseconds and smaller as the abilities get larger.

My craft is making technology work for humans, allowing humans more time to be creative and present with one another. I love living in this unprecedented shift in technology and want to continue guiding us in a good way.

[6] https://www.youtube.com/@peterdiamandis

## What’s a controversial or unpopular opinion you hold about software engineering, and why?*

Any opinion is probably controversial to someone; as recently as last year I heard a founder poo-pooing the use of AI in writing code; now it seems inevitable that AI will be writing languages and compilers.
