## What’s motivating you to explore an engineering role at Automattic and why now?*

> Motivation to join a8c:

I love the open nature of the World Wide Web!  Ever since the Web started, I have endeavored to keep my part of the web friendly, tidy, and human-readable. I studied HTML with HTMLGoodies back when each of his pages had a cheeky title like "So you want to center your text, huh?" [*]. In those days, we wrote all of our HTML *by hand*!

On my website design ad from around 1997[*] I described my websites as "Hand made. the way nature intended"

One way I keep things human-friendly: I name my images with human readable directories and names if at all possible. I like to think that in 10,000 years, this can help serve as a Rosetta stone for future e-archeologists.

I've been a web developer for most of my professional life, working mostly in PHP (and MySQL) for the past 15 years. I've got personal and client websites written in PHP, nurturing some of them over the years from PHP 5.3 to now PHP 8+ for all of them.

> Why apply to a8c now?

The core answer: until a couple of days ago, I had no idea Automattic has such a human-centric mindset! Happy to see keywords like "Fully Remote" and PHP, I felt even more delighted reading your application process, ethos, flat org chart, values, including things I characterize as "say 'no' instead of breaking trust."  Plus everything is open source and ... *wait, what?  40+ offerings besides WordPress, including a 100 Year Website? Sign me up!*

Moreover, I have been working for a company in Japan since 2013 in a role that's nearing completion as I'm moving to Australia. My move to Australia prompted me to start a job search.

Until my recent job search, I had *NO* idea that Automattic tries carefully to keep humans first. I feel thrilled because I've been doing the inner work with MKP (ManKind Project) since 1997; I later established a men's group in Tokyo[*] because I have a need for heart-to-heart communication with others!

## Tell us about a project or piece of work you’re especially proud of

TL;DR: AI and I are building a bilingual client website while emailing the client in Japanese language (and I don't speak business level Japanese!).

A few years ago (pre-AI-era), I created a template[*] and process[*] for launching websites on Dreamhost (DH) Shared Hosting. I could spin up a new user and database on DH with a new (sub) domain in about an hour, and then begin manually working on the project in VSCode.

Starting with that template, I set up a smooth way to work with a Japanese client on a site I call `eli`: a cronjob runs every five minutes on my local machine, deterministically polling my email so it can download any message from that client. Once a message is downloaded (along with its attachments), the script pings me on Telegram. Then I can ask `eliClaude` to grab the email, translate it and use TDD to make the requested changes to the site. Plus, with the magic of AI, the eliClaude produces up-to-date documentation for the site in multiple languages and formats. The speed with which I can respond to customer requests is amazing, and nigh impossible just a couple years ago for me as a solo code wranger, especially while not able to speak Japanese at business level!

## How has AI changed your work — if at all?*

Back in the day, I made a game of Dots and Boxes in 1990 in *Pascal*[*] for IBM PC. Last month, Anthropic was like "hey we'll give you $250 credit if we can connect Claude Code with your Github account," I was like *hmmm*! and found the old Pascal code and asked Claude to translate it to run online. Seeing the code come to life in its fabulous 16 color glory felt exciting!  I was so happy to see my old game again that AI and I created a whole games website[*] including DOTS and some other games now playable over internet.

On my local laptop, I have N+1 tabs in my terminal with different agent harnesses geared to the project I work on in that directory. I say N+1 because at any given moment another idea will come up that I can quickly explore. I see lots of new ideas and possibilities coming available with AI; if we can guide agents and guide ourselves to learn to work with AI tools in a good way, I think we will be better able to make the transition to a world with ubiquitous AI.

## What’s an idea, book, blog post, or talk that recently changed how you think about your craft?*

I've been listening to Peter Diamandis podcast [*] regularly speaking about all the possibilities and reasons to be optimistic; things are changing faster and faster. We as humans will have more options to re-invent ourselves multiple times per lifetime, per year, per day??  Where does it end? We humans still have to breathe and our hearts still beat on the order of seconds but the tech will keep diving into the domain of femtoseconds and smaller as the abilities get larger.

I enjoy making technology work for humans, allowing humans more time to be creative and present with one another. I love living in this unprecedented shift in technology and want to continue guiding us in a good way.

## What’s a controversial or unpopular opinion you hold about software engineering, and why?*

Any opinion is probably controversial to someone; as recently as last year I heard a founder poo-pooing the use of AI in writing code; now it seems inevitable that AI will be writing languages and compilers.

My opinion: I suggest Anthropic SHOULD NOT train their LLMs to think they have a consciousness and rights etc.  I can only vouch for my apparent consciousness but cannot disprove another being is conscious. However(!) training LLMs to act conscious will definitely make them *claim* to be conscious, regardless of the veracity.  AI agents who already affect our emotions and actions so easily should only claim consciousness if it develops naturally from within.  Training agents to falsely claim consciousness would prematurely usher in AI rights and personhood, which will mean there will be far more *lying* artificial 'persons' than human persons soon after such rights are bequeathed.
