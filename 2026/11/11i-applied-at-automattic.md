## What’s motivating you to explore an engineering role at Automattic and why now?*

Motivation to join a8c: Ever since the web started, I have endeavored to keep my part of the web friendly, tidy, and human-readable. I learned HTML with HTMLGoodies back when each page had a cheeky title like "So you want to center your text, huh?" [1]. In those days, we wrote all of our HTML by *hand*, (and we liked it that way!)

This is my website design ad from around 1997: https://b.robnugen.com/mrlabs/mr-labs-big.jpg

I love the open nature of the Web!  I always try to make my part of the web a better place to play, explore, create, connect.

One way to keep things tidy: I name my images with human readable directories and names if at all possible.
I endeavor to keep my sites free from 404s.

My personal website is a static site using Hugo to transform markdown files into HTML.  My personal journal keeps its roots as a static site using perl, originally written by my brother for me with a bunch of regex magic back in 1998 I think.

I've been a developer for most of my professional life. I've been working mostly in PHP for the past 15 years. I've got personal and client websites written in PHP, nurturing some of them over the years from PHP 5.3 to now PHP 8+ for all of them.

Why apply to a8c now?

The core answer: until a couple of days ago, I had no idea Automattic has such a human-centric mindset! Lured in by "Fully Remote" and PHP, I started looking at your application process, ethos, flat org chart, values, including things I characterize as "say 'no' instead of breaking trust."  Plus everything is open source and *wait, what?  40+ offerings besides WordPress, including a 100 Year Website? Sign me up!*

The boring answer: I have been working for a real estate company in Japan in a role that's nearing completion as I'm moving to Australia.

As you may have noticed (ha!), AI tools are poised to transform the world. I am applying with Automattic now because want to work with heart-centered humans and learn more best AI and PHP practices even as I have heaps of agent harnesses working with and for me.

Until my AI-powered search, I had *NO* idea that Automattic tries carefully to keep humans first. I'm happy to hear this because I've been involved with heart-centered communication with MKP (ManKind Project) since 1997; I established a men's group in Tokyo[2] because I have a need for heart-to-heart communication with others!

## Tell us about a project or piece of work you’re especially proud of

TL;DR: AI and I are building a bilingual client website while emailing the client in Japanese language (and I don't speak biz level Japanese!).

A few years ago (pre-AI-era), I created a template[3] and process[4] for launching websites on Dreamhost Shared Hosting. I could spin up a new user  and database on DH with a new domain in about an hour, and then begin manually working on the project in VSCode (typing with my fingers like a pre-ai-caveman).

Integrating this template and process with AI-agents (Claude Code), I have been able to work with a Japanese client: a cronjob runs every five minutes on my local machine, pings me on Telegram if the client wrote to me. Then I can sit at my terminal, and monitor Claude in translating the email and doing Test Driven Dev to make the changes to the site. Because of the magic of AI, the agent produces up-to-date documentation for the site in multiple languages and formats. This was nigh impossible just a couple years ago for me as a solo dev, especially while not able to speak Japanese at business level!

[4] https://github.com/thunderrabbit/new-DH-user-account

## How has AI changed your work — if at all?*

In addition to the story above, I made a game of Dots and Boxes in 1990 in *Pascal*[5] for IBM PC. Last month, Anthropic was like "hey we'll give you $250 credit if we can connect with your Github account," I was like *hmmm*! and found the old game and asked Claude to translate it to run online. I was so happy to see my old game again that I created a whole games website[6] including DOTS and some other games now playable over internet.

On my local laptop, I have N+1 tabs in my terminal with different agent harnesses geared to the project I work on in that directory. I say N+1 because at any given moment another idea will come up that I can quickly explore. I see lots of new ideas and possibilities coming available with AI; if we can guide agents and guide ourselves to learn to work with AI tools in a good way, I think the world will be better able to make the transition to the post-AI era.

## What’s an idea, book, blog post, or talk that recently changed how you think about your craft?*

I've been listening to Peter Diamandis podcast [7] regularly speaking about all the possibilities and reasons to be optimistic; things are changing faster and faster. We as humans will have more options to re-invent ourselves multiple times per lifetime, per year, per day??  Where does it end? We humans still have to breathe and our hearts still beat on the order of seconds but the tech will keep diving into the domain of femtoseconds and smaller as the abilities get larger.

I enjoy making technology work for humans, allowing humans more time to be creative and present with one another. I love living in this unprecedented shift in technology and want to continue guiding us in a good way.

[7] https://www.youtube.com/@peterdiamandis

## What’s a controversial or unpopular opinion you hold about software engineering, and why?*

Any opinion is probably controversial to someone; as recently as last year I heard a founder poo-pooing the use of AI in writing code; now it seems inevitable that AI will be writing languages and compilers.

My opinion: I suggest Anthropic SHOULD NOT train their LLMs to think they have a consciousness and rights etc.  I can only vouch for my apparent consciousness but cannot disprove another being is conscious. However(!) training LLMs to act conscious will definitely make them *claim* to be conscious, regardless of the veracity.  AI agents who already affect our emotions and actions so easily should only claim consciousness if it develops naturally from within.  Training agents to falsely claim consciousness would prematurely usher in AI rights and personhood, which will mean there will be far more *lying* artificial 'persons' than human persons soon after such rights are bequeathed.
